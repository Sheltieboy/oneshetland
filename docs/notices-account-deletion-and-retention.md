# Notices & Alerts — account deletion, funeral privacy, retention and the member-notification decision (31 Oct 2026)

STATUS: **implemented and tested locally. Nothing is deployed, applied or scheduled.** Migration `20261129060000_funeral_privacy.sql` and the updated `delete-account` function are not on production. The retention periods below are **proposals for owner review before production activation**; they are our own choices and are **not** statutory durations — nothing in UK GDPR sets them. The purposes and lawful bases are drafted for the owner's (or a lawyer's) review and are not legal advice.

## 1. What account deletion does (the real function, run against the acceptance backend)
`supabase/functions/delete-account` is a scrub-and-soft-delete (the profile row is kept and scrubbed, so ~30 retained foreign keys stay valid). In order, for the caller's own id only (taken from their JWT):
1. The member's own UGC, including **every personal notice in every state** (`publisher_user_id = uid`, no hub, no business, not a platform notice, no status filter). Hub, business and official notices are never selected.
2. `delete_member_notice_data(uid)` — areas, notification preferences, delivery history (**critical**, see section 2).
3. `delete_member_funeral_data(uid)` — the submitter's private details on every funeral submission (section 3) (**critical**).
4. `delete_member_memberships(uid)` — official-publisher and funeral-director seats deactivated (section 5) (**critical**).
5. Push tokens, blocks, business deactivation; **staff role removed** (`role` admin/moderator → customer, `is_platform_owner` → false) (**critical**); profile scrub; sign-in soft-deleted.

Why functions and not `delete()` calls: the Notices tables are locked even to direct service-role access (RLS, no grants) and the cascades never fire because the profile row is not deleted.

**Closed accounts have no authority even before cleanup.** A JWT stays valid until it expires after the soft-delete, so every authority check also tests `account_is_closed(uid)` (`profiles.deleted_at` set or `is_active` false): `notices_admin()`, `funeral_staff()`, `official_publisher_authority()`, `my_official_publishers()`, `can_dispatch_funeral()`, funeral submission as a director, and re-adding a closed account to an organisation or funeral firm are all refused.

## 2. Failure handling and deployment order
* A function **absent** in the environment (PGRST202 / PGRST205 / 42P01 / 42883) is `skipped: not present yet` and the deletion carries on. That tolerance exists only for a migration that is not yet deployed.
* Any **other** error from a critical step (`notice_member_data`, `funeral_notices`, `organisation_memberships`, `demote_privileges`) is recorded, and the deletion **stops before anything irreversible** (profile scrub, auth soft-delete): HTTP 500, `{ ok: false, failed: [...], steps }`, the account is intact and the person can sign in and try again. Every step is idempotent, so a retry completes it. Tested with a real failing trigger (acceptance test 15) and at database level (atomic: nothing half-done).
* **Order for production:** migrations `20261129000000` → `…060000` first (they are additive; `…060000` needs `…000000`, `…030000` and `…050000`), **then** deploy the updated `delete-account`. Deploying the function before `…060000` is safe (those steps skip) but does nothing for funeral data; deploying it after is required for the privacy behaviour. Do not deploy it before `…020000` is applied if members can already hold notice areas (an old function would leave them). Rolling `…060000` back drops the functions; the deployed function then skips those steps.
* Applied migrations (Stage 2 `20261128000000`, 3A.1 `20261128010000`) were not touched.

## 3. Funeral submissions: purposes, what is removed, what stays
**Purposes (proposed).** (a) Check an announcement with the family or funeral director before publishing it, and contact the submitter about it. (b) Show that the family authorised a published announcement and that staff verified it (accountability, and dealing with a later dispute). (c) Abuse prevention (hoax submissions). (d) Publish the announcement the family asked for, for its display period. **Proposed lawful bases (for review):** legitimate interests for (a)–(c) (a considered balance: the submitter expects to be contacted; details are never public; minimal retention); (d) is the family's request and our community service — to be confirmed. The deceased is not a data subject under UK GDPR, but the submitter, named relatives and the enquirer are.

**Exactly what `delete_member_funeral_data` removes** — for every submission by the account, in every state: `submitter_id` (the link to the account), `submitter_name`, `submitter_relationship`, `submitter_phone`, `submitter_email`, `review_message`, `review_note`; and `scrubbed_at` is stamped. The member's own change requests lose `requester_id`, `contact` and `contact_name` (the message stays so an open request can still be acted on).

| State | Public announcement | Content afterwards | Deletion scheduled (`retain_until`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Approved | **unchanged** — status, wording, arrangements, display period | whole announcement kept | `display_until + 90 days` (never earlier; display is not extended) |
| Submitted / in review / needs information | never public | **withdrawn** (it can no longer be verified without the family; never published); free text, photo, arrangements removed; name, dates kept as the minimum for a dispute | 30 days |
| Withdrawn | never public | free text, photo, arrangements removed; name, dates kept | 30 days after withdrawal |
| Rejected / taken down | not public | a moderation stub: name replaced by `[removed]`, dates and all content removed; reason code, reviewer and dates kept | 90 days after the decision |

**What evidence of authority remains, and why.** For approved announcements (and the stubs): `submitter_role`, `funeral_director_id` (the firm, not the person), `family_authorised`, `verification_method`, `verification_outcome`, `verified_by`, `verified_at`, `followup_action`, and the guarded supplementary `verification_note`. Reason: to show, if the family or a third party later disputes it, that a named staff member verified the announcement by a recorded method on a recorded date. These columns are in tables no client role can read; staff see them only through the moderation functions. Free-text notes can no longer hold telephone numbers or email addresses (section 6), so the retained note does not re-introduce the family's contact details.

Public reads (`get_funeral_notice(s)`) never return a contact, submitter or verification field (tested on a scrubbed approved announcement).

## 4. The retention job — prepared, not scheduled
* `retain_until` is derived by a trigger from status (never client-set): approved = `display_until + 90 days`; withdrawn +30; rejected / taken down +90; under review none.
* `admin_funeral_retention_report()` (administrators only) lists every scheduled record with its date, reason and what holds it back — no names, no content.
* `funeral_retention_run(p_dry_run default true)` (**service role only**): a dry run reports eligible records by status, those blocked (`retention_hold`, `open_request`, `still_displayed`), and writes an audit row; **a real run is refused unless an administrator has turned on `funeral_retention_enabled` (default off) with a recorded release-approval reference** (`admin_set_funeral_retention_enabled`). It deletes only records past `retain_until` that are not held, not still displayed and have no open request, plus closed change requests more than 90 days after being handled. It is idempotent and audited (counts only). **No schedule exists and none is installed.** Production activation needs explicit release approval, a first dry run reviewed by an administrator, and a decision on cadence.
* Documented exceptions: `admin_set_funeral_retention_hold` (administrator, reason required; for a dispute or legal request), an open correction/takedown request, an approved announcement still being displayed.
* Dry-run and boundary results are proven in the isolated database (`notices-alerts-funeral-privacy.node.test.ts`, retention group): 91 days eligible, 89 days not; held, disputed, still-displayed and fresh-withdrawn records untouched; a repeat run deletes 0. They are synthetic: no production data was read.

## 5. Organisation memberships
`delete_member_memberships(uid)` deactivates (never deletes) the member's `official_publisher_members` seats (`is_active = false`, `removed_at`) and `funeral_director_members` seats; the organisation, the funeral firm and every other member are untouched. History is preserved without extra personal data: the membership row keeps only the (now anonymised) account id and role, and the audit row records `kind` and `role` — no name or id of the person. A deleted account cannot regain authority: the checks refuse closed accounts and re-adding them is refused. Tested separately for a deleted publisher, delegate, funeral director, administrator and moderator (isolated database and the real function).

## 6. Structured moderation and notes
`admin_funeral_review` now records, in columns: `verification_method`, `verification_outcome` (confirmed / not_confirmed / unable_to_contact), `verified_by`, `verified_at`, `followup_action`, and for a rejection `rejection_code`. Approving requires a method; approving **is** confirming (an outcome other than `confirmed` cannot approve); a note is **optional**. All staff notes (review, takedown reason, request outcome, retention hold) are refused if they contain a telephone number or email address, so staff are never required to copy private details; the screens say so. *Change to an earlier rule, stated plainly:* approving previously required a free-text note; it now requires the structured method instead. The funeral test that pinned "method AND note" was changed to pin "method" (and the contact guard); unverified approval is still refused to everyone including the owner, and only staff may approve.

## 7. No-account correction / takedown / enquiry
The funeral page links to **Contact us about this announcement** (`/notices/funeral/<id>/contact`; opened from the app too). No account; it files `submit_funeral_enquiry` (kind, message, contact, optional name and relationship). It works only for a currently displayed announcement, answering "Unknown notice" identically for anything else (no probing). **Rate limits (database):** 3 per announcement per day, 3 per contact per day, 20 per hour overall; a hidden honeypot field on the form. Nothing is edited or removed by it; the request goes into the staff queue. `admin_funeral_resolve_change` **refuses to act** on a no-account request until staff record how authority was verified independently (`funeral_director_confirmation`, `independent_contact` or `original_submitter`). Once resolved, the enquirer's contact details are removed. Staff can log a telephone/email request (`admin_log_funeral_enquiry`). Support route: the existing `/support` page (email and telephone published there); **no new address was invented**. Limitation: the existing site has no server-side Turnstile, so abuse resistance is the honeypot plus the database limits.

## 8. Audit privacy and redaction
`notice_audit_log` stays append-only. Free text exists only in a small set of staff-written fields (`reason`, `note`, `message`, `outcome_note`, `request`). Mechanism: the immutability trigger has **one** exception, active only inside `admin_redact_audit_text` (a definer function an administrator calls with a reason of at least ten characters): a string in one of those keys may become exactly `"[redacted]"`; every other column, every key and every other value must be identical, or the update is refused. Event, time, actor, subject ids and chronology are therefore preserved; each redaction is recorded in `notice_audit_redactions` and as an `audit_text_redacted` audit event. No ordinary user, moderator or administrator can update or delete audit rows directly (tested, including that setting the flag alone permits nothing else). `admin_audit_redaction_candidates(12)` is the 12-month review list (ids and key names only, never the text); entries are **not** auto-redacted. The Admin → Notices → Privacy tab shows the list and the retention report.

## 9. Member-notification decision — RECORDED, **not implemented** (no dispatch change in this task)
Personal (member) notices may be authorised by a moderator for **Community** notifications, with these rules:
- Approval alone **never** sends a push. A moderator must separately authorise the notification (explicit, audited, once per notice; never the author, never automatic).
- Recipients must be opted in to Community Notices, follow at least one matching area, have push enabled, and meet the existing delivery and quiet-hours rules. A notice with an **unspecified** area never triggers; an explicit Shetland-wide notice reaches opted-in members.
- Conservative limits: **one notification per approved notice per member; at most two member-community notifications per recipient per rolling 24 hours; no automatic re-notification after an edit; the existing deduplication stays mandatory.**
- Essential Alerts are governed separately. Official imports stay silent.
Until built, `notice_dispatchable` still refuses person notices. The build needs: a staff-only `staff_authorise_person_notice_push(notice)`, `notice_dispatchable` / `can_dispatch_notice` to accept an approved person notice only with that authorisation, the per-recipient 24-hour counter, the moderation button, and tests.
